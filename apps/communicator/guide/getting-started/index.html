Usage guide

Getting started

What Sono is, what to think about before the first session, and how the first-run setup works.

On this page

  1. What is Sono?
  2. Before you begin
  3. First-run setup, step by step

What is Sono? # — Copy link to this section

Sono is a free communication app for people who don't speak, speak only a little, or can't rely on speech. The person taps picture symbols and Sono says them out loud with the chosen voice. In the Advanced and Menu layouts, several symbols can be joined on the sentence strip and spoken as one sentence; in the Simple layout, every tap speaks one word.

This way of communicating is called augmentative and alternative communication (AAC). It covers everything from gestures and paper picture boards to apps like this one, and many people use several of these side by side. Sono is one tool among them: an app for everyday communication, not a healthcare service. It gives no medical advice, diagnosis or treatment, and it doesn't replace an assessment by a speech and language therapist or another communication specialist. Nothing in this guide is medical advice.

Sources

  • ASHA / CHSA — Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC): information for the public
  • ISAAC — What is AAC?

Before you begin # — Copy link to this section

Every person is different, and professional guidance is clear that a communication system should be matched to the individual, not the other way round. Before the first session, note down:

  • How the person communicates now: pointing, leading you by the hand, gestures, sounds, facial expressions, eye gaze.
  • What they understand when spoken to, and which words or routines they react to.
  • How comfortably they can see, reach and tap a screen, and for how long.
  • Who they communicate with most, and where: home, school, grandparents, therapy.
  • What they love: foods, toys, games, people, songs. These become the first words.

These notes help you choose a layout, a grid size and a starting vocabulary. If a speech and language therapist already works with the person, ask them to help with these choices and to show you how they use communication aids, so that home and therapy pull in the same direction.

Sources

  • ASHA — Augmentative and Alternative Communication (Practice Portal)
  • Communication Matters — About AAC systems
  • RCSLT — Augmentative and alternative communication (AAC)

First-run setup, step by step # — Copy link to this section

When Sono opens for the first time, tap Start Communicating and answer the storage question. Setup then has four short pages. You can tap Skip at any time and change everything later in Caretaker Settings.

  1. About you: pick an accent colour and a light or dark appearance, choose the picture style for the people illustrations (Neutral, Male or Female) and choose the voice under Voice & Language. The voice sets the language of the whole app: the interface, the symbol names and the speech. Confirm the user agreement to continue.
  2. Make it yours: choose a board layout, Advanced, Simple or Menu. A live preview shows the real board, and Try it full-screen lets you tap around before deciding. See "Board layouts and tap behaviour" under Caretaker settings for who each one suits.
  3. Fine-tune: set the grid size (fewer columns means bigger buttons), the symbol style (Card or Circle), the tap behaviour (whether one tap or a double tap adds a word to the sentence) and whether all text is shown in uppercase.
  4. Quick Tips: a short summary, then Done.

The board then opens with a one-time tour: Sono points at the sentence strip, the categories, the symbols and the settings gear, and speaks each step aloud. Tap Next to follow it, or Skip tour to go straight to the board.
