Usage guide

Tabs, categories and colours

How the vocabulary is organised: the special tabs, the thirteen categories with their subcategories, and what the colours mean.

On this page

  1. My Symbols, Recents and Start
  2. Categories and subcategories
  3. Colour coding

My Symbols, Recents and Start # — Copy link to this section

In the Advanced and Simple layouts the tab row runs: My Symbols, Recents, Start, then the categories, then Activities and Learning. Swipe left or right across the symbols to move between tabs; the tab's name is spoken as it opens.

  • My Symbols holds the symbols you created yourself, with your own photos.
  • Recents keeps the last 30 different symbols used, newest first, so favourite words stay close at hand. Hidden symbols drop out of it, and it can be cleared under Caretaker Settings > Message History.
  • Start holds sentence starters such as "I want…", "I need…", "I feel…", "Where is…" and "It hurts…". Tapping one puts its words on the sentence strip, says it, and jumps to the category where the sentence usually continues. Switch starters off, or add your own, under Caretaker Settings > Sentence Starters.

The Menu layout works differently: the whole screen is a menu of category tiles, plus My Symbols, Activities and Learning. Tap a tile to open it; the Home button or the Back gesture returns to the menu. Menu has no Recents or Start tile.

Categories and subcategories # — Copy link to this section

The built-in categories, in the order they appear, are People, Basics, Feelings, Actions, Greetings, Manners, Food, Places, Things, Time, Animals, 123s and ABCs. Inside a category, subcategory chips narrow it down; in the Menu layout the first chip, All, shows everything.

For the first words, Basics and Manners hold the most useful ones: yes, no, help, more, stop, all done, please, thank you.

Under Caretaker Settings > Categories you can switch built-in categories off so the board shows fewer tabs; a category also disappears on its own when all of its symbols are hidden. Restore all brings everything back.

Colour coding # — Copy link to this section

Each category has its own colour, and every symbol tile carries its category's colour in its border. Over time this helps the person find their way around: food is always orange, places always teal, animals always green.

Symbols you create yourself get a word type instead: People, Actions, Things, Feelings, Questions, Basics or Important, following the Modified Fitzgerald Key, a colour system that groups words by their role in a sentence. In the Advanced layout, My Symbols shows a filter row in those colours once your symbols span more than one word type.
