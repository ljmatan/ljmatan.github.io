Does the app work offline? # — Copy link to this section

Yes. The board, the sentence strip, the games and the drawing board need no internet. Speech works offline too: Sono uses the voice installed on the device, or a neural voice you have added yourself. You need a connection only to download lessons in the Learning tab (they play offline afterwards), to download a voice, and to open the legal documents.

One privacy note: when the device's own voice is used, the device maker's speech service may send the spoken text to its servers, as some of them do. Adding an offline voice avoids that; the Privacy Policy has the details.

Can I use it on more than one device? # — Copy link to this section

Yes. Install Sono on as many devices as you need. There is no account and no cloud: settings, custom symbols, photos, recordings and history stay on each device. To carry your custom symbols and settings to another device, use Caretaker Settings > Backup & Restore: export on one device, import on the other.

What age is Sono for? # — Copy link to this section

There is no fixed age. Professional guidance says augmentative and alternative communication is for anyone of any age, from toddlers to adults, and that nobody is "too young" to start. Adjust the grid size, the layout and the picture style to the person. Sono is caretaker-managed: the settings sit behind the gear and its number question.

Which languages are supported? # — Copy link to this section

Sono speaks 14 languages with 15 voices: English (US and GB), German, Italian, French, Spanish, Croatian, Bosnian, Serbian, Montenegrin, Czech, Polish, Finnish, Slovak and Danish. The language follows the voice: choose it during setup or later under Caretaker Settings > Speech & Voice, and the interface, the symbol names and the speech switch together. On a fresh install Sono picks the device's language when it speaks it.

By default Sono speaks with the voice installed on the device for that language. If the device has no voice for it, Sono says so and uses an English voice until you install one in the device's text-to-speech settings. Croatian, Bosnian, Serbian and Montenegrin always use the device's voice, and can borrow each other's.

For the other languages you can add an offline neural voice: in Speech & Voice turn on Allow custom on-device models, then tap Improve voice next to the voice and add a voice file from a link, from a file, or from the download page it points to.

Will using pictures stop them from talking? # — Copy link to this section

This is the worry parents raise most often, and it has been studied. A research review looked at what happens to speech when augmentative and alternative communication is introduced and found that in none of the 27 best-documented cases did speech decrease; most gained some speech, usually modestly. A review of studies with autistic children came to the same conclusion, and so did a randomised trial with toddlers who had fewer than ten words, where the children whose parents used symbols together with speech ended up with larger vocabularies than those who heard speech alone. ASHA lists "using symbols reduces the motivation to speak" among the misconceptions it works to correct.

These are results across groups, not a promise for one person, and the speech gains in those studies were modest. What the evidence does say is that offering symbols is not giving up on speech: it gives the person a way to be understood today while everything else continues.

Is Sono a therapy? # — Copy link to this section

No. Sono is an app for everyday communication, not a healthcare service. It gives no medical advice, diagnosis or treatment, it hasn't been clinically evaluated or certified for clinical use, and it is not meant for emergencies: make sure the people around the person know the other ways they show that something is wrong. This guide shares general information and the published guidance it links to; decisions about one person belong with them, their family and their speech and language therapist or doctor.
